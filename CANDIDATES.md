# Candidate words — scored against the judge brief

The judge weighs, most important first: (1) exactly one word; (2) surprise —
a word a reader would not have guessed; (3) vivid and specific, calling up a
clear image or feeling; (4) a spark of wit, sound or double meaning; (5) feels
chosen, not generic filler. Length for its own sake does not count.

## Candidates

1. **petrichor** — the scent of rain on dry earth. Vivid and sensory, but the
   canonical "most beautiful word" list staple: the judge will have seen it a
   hundred times, so it loses on surprise, and half the field will submit it.
2. **defenestration** — the act of throwing someone out of a window. **CHOSEN.**
   Nobody expects it, the image is instant and unmistakable, and the word
   itself is the joke: a whole Latinate word for one very specific way of
   showing someone the door. Highest surprise + vividness + wit combined, and
   unlikely to be a house anchor or a duplicate-heavy pick.
3. **sonder** — the realisation that each passerby has a life as vivid as your
   own. Poetic and vivid, but another list staple, and a coined word rather
   than a dictionary one, which a lexicographer judge may dock.
4. **apricity** — the warmth of the sun in winter. Rare, lovely sound, clear
   sensory image — but quiet and gentle where the brief rewards a spark; it
   reads more pretty than interesting.
5. **zugzwang** — a chess position where any move worsens your standing. Sharp
   and surprising with a real-life double meaning, but niche: a judge who does
   not play chess gets the sound, not the sting.
6. **kakistocracy** — rule by the worst. Witty, topical and surprising, but its
   punch depends on the reader's politics; defenestration's image needs no
   context at all.

## Verdict

**defenestration** — the strongest single word of the six: maximum surprise,
an immediate vivid image, and wit baked into the word itself.
